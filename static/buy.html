The rules that would make it trustworthy

  1. 01Pre-registered claim. The wording and the timeframe are published before anyone is measured. No rewriting the claim to fit the result.
  2. 02Noise-gated. A user counts only if their change is larger than their own measured photo noise.
  3. 03Confirmation rule. Beyond noise at two check-ins in a row, same direction. One lucky reading is not a result.
  4. 04Minimum n. No badge until enough consenting users have passed the timeframe. The count is always printed.
  5. 05No cherry-picking. Everyone who started is in the denominator, including people who got worse or stopped. Aggregates only, never photos.

Built on YouCam. YouCam’s quality gates (face size, face angle) reject bad photos up front. SkinProof adds per-person noise and confirmation, so YouCam’s measurements are strong enough to act on.

This is a proposal. No brand uses it, nothing here is a live feature, and the badge above shows invented numbers. A real version needs a pilot, consent, and review by the brand’s regulatory team. See docs/IMPACT.md in the repository for the evidence behind the rules.

SkinProof is not a medical device and does not diagnose. A measured change is not proof the product caused it.
